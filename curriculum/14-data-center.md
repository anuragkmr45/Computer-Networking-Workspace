# M14 — Data-center fabrics and storage connectivity

**Status:** curriculum specification; not a claim that lessons have been completed.
**Prerequisites:** M09, M12, M13

## Topic scope
- **M14.01** — Leaf-spine topology, ECMP, and capacity planning
- **M14.02** — Underlay/overlay separation and failure visibility
- **M14.03** — VXLAN/EVPN concepts and tenant isolation
- **M14.04** — Anycast gateways, multi-homing, and control-plane state
- **M14.05** — Storage-networking foundations and traffic characteristics
- **M14.06** — Multi-site fabric extension and failure-domain trade-offs
- **M14.07** — Oversubscription, hotspots, and telemetry
- **M14.08** — Fabric migrations and operational validation

## Theory and mechanisms
Explain forwarding and control state across an overlay without losing sight of the underlay path.

## Numericals and algorithmic work
Calculate simplified oversubscription, bisection/cut capacity, and encapsulation overhead with a drawn topology.

## Practical route
Design first; add a small approved emulation later. Do not claim vendor configuration compatibility without version checks.

## Senior/principal application
Compare L2 extension against routed boundaries and justify a failure-containment strategy.

## Evidence to collect
Ask for a mechanism explanation and an unfamiliar application. Add calculations, state traces, experiments, troubleshooting, and a design rationale when applicable. Record what was attempted, assistance, observations, and remaining gaps. Untested dimensions stay unassessed; watching a video or reading a generated note is not mastery.

## Lesson expansion rule
Prepare detailed lessons only as needed. Record stable topic IDs in notes and evidence. Use source dates and version-specific documentation for implementations. A learner may request a useful specialization without completing every unrelated branch.

## Sources to consult
CISCO-CCIE, JUNIPER-TRACKS in [the source register](../references/SOURCES.md). These are starting references, not proof that each listed topic is fully covered by a single source. Retrieve topic-specific standards and vendor documentation before advanced teaching.
