# M16 — Principal-level design and operational leadership

**Status:** curriculum specification; not a claim that lessons have been completed.
**Prerequisites:** M08, M11, M12, M13

## Topic scope
- **M16.01** — Requirements, stakeholder needs, and measurable success
- **M16.02** — Availability goals, failure domains, and correlated risks
- **M16.03** — Capacity forecasting, headroom, and cost models
- **M16.04** — Security, compliance interfaces, and operational ownership
- **M16.05** — Architecture alternatives and decision records
- **M16.06** — Migration, compatibility, rollback, and incident readiness
- **M16.07** — Cross-team communication, review, and technical strategy
- **M16.08** — Capstone: end-to-end hybrid platform design and failure review

## Theory and mechanisms
Turn incomplete requirements into testable designs and communicate why one option is preferable under explicit constraints.

## Numericals and algorithmic work
Calculate capacity/headroom and availability budgets; avoid unjustified independent-failure assumptions.

## Practical route
Use capstones/README.md for a design, an incident exercise, and a tested change plan.

## Senior/principal application
Present alternatives, limits, failure recovery, observability, and measurable acceptance criteria.

## Evidence to collect
Ask for a mechanism explanation and an unfamiliar application. Add calculations, state traces, experiments, troubleshooting, and a design rationale when applicable. Record what was attempted, assistance, observations, and remaining gaps. Untested dimensions stay unassessed; watching a video or reading a generated note is not mastery.

## Lesson expansion rule
Prepare detailed lessons only as needed. Record stable topic IDs in notes and evidence. Use source dates and version-specific documentation for implementations. A learner may request a useful specialization without completing every unrelated branch.

## Sources to consult
AWS-VPC, K8S-NETWORK, CISCO-CCIE in [the source register](../references/SOURCES.md). These are starting references, not proof that each listed topic is fully covered by a single source. Retrieve topic-specific standards and vendor documentation before advanced teaching.
